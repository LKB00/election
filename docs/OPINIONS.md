# Keep the ink, widen the ballot

Election can become "the place to see what people think about anything" without throwing away what makes it special. The plan has three parts. First, **rename the product and change its words** from "election duel" to plain "poll" or "question". Second, **keep one or two Indian voting rituals as the signature**, mainly the inked finger and the "guess the crowd" moment, and move the rest (EVM beep, VVPAT slip, voter ID, counting day) into an optional "Election mode". Third, **add new question types and a real feed** so there is a reason to come back every day. This matters because your real rival is not another website. It is WhatsApp's own free poll, which already has deadlines and secret voting ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/01/17/whatsapp-launches-voice-updates-and-polls-for-channels), [Techlusive](https://www.techlusive.in/news/whatsapp-announces-new-group-chat-features-poll-deadlines-all-mentions-and-more-1670703/)). You win only on what WhatsApp lacks: one link that works across many groups, results from strangers as well as friends, good share images, and the reveal moment. The code change is mostly *additive*. Election features become a switch on each poll instead of the whole product. The biggest job is adding new question types (about 1–2 weeks of developer work). The rebrand alone is a few days. Some legal work is a must-do whatever you choose: a terms page, a named grievance officer, fast takedowns, and keeping the "results sealed during real elections" feature. *These legal points come from research notes and are not legal advice. Have an Indian tech lawyer check them once before you grow.*

## The word "Election" boxes you into politics and election season

A brand built on "election" gives people no reason to visit between elections. This is the same trap Koo fell into as "India's Twitter". Koo grew fast in 2021 when ministers joined it. It reached about 9M monthly users. Then it shut in July 2024. A well-known critic said it was "almost a copy-paste of Twitter" with nothing new to offer ([MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2024/07/223-koo-shutdown-lessons-to-learn/), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/07/02/indian-social-network-koo-to-shut-down)). The words are also legally loaded. **Section 126A of the Representation of the People Act** makes it a crime to run an exit poll or publish its results "in any other manner whatsoever" while the Election Commission has a ban in force. The penalty is up to 2 years in jail ([Lawyered](https://www.lawyered.in/legal-disrupt/articles/exit-poll-legal/), [ECI notification](https://elections24.eci.gov.in/docs/RF5k9XoTAd.pdf)). The research found no rule against using "election" as a brand name. Still, a name that sounds like an official election body, or a feature called "Exit poll", invites trouble you do not need.

**Naming.** Apps that spread fast use short, chatty words that sound like slang or a feeling: tbh ("to be honest"), Gas, Koo, Moj ("fun" in Hindi/Urdu) ([Wikipedia: Gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas_(app)), [Wikipedia: Moj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moj)). Good directions for you are Hinglish words about choosing or giving your view, such as *raay* (opinion), *bolo* (say) or *chuno* (choose). These are directions only. Nobody has checked them for domains, Play Store clashes or trademarks yet. Do those three checks before you decide. Tagline ideas to test in your WhatsApp groups (none are tested yet): "Har baat pe vote", "Sabki raay, ek link mein", "Bolo, kaun jeetega?". Stay away from the Ashoka emblem, a tricolour logo, ECI-style seals and the word "official". The Emblems and Names Act limits commercial use of some national names and symbols ([IndiaCode](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/1896/1/aA1950-12.pdf)).

**What to keep from the election idea.** Products that last own one small ritual, not a whole theme. Examples: BeReal's one daily alert, Gas's "flames", and YouGov's "see how you compare" ([Wikipedia: BeReal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BeReal), [The Ways to Wealth](https://www.thewaystowealth.com/reviews/yougov-review/)). The **inked finger** is the best signature you have. It is uniquely Indian, it belongs to no party, it feels happy, and it fits any topic ("I voted on Biryani vs Pulao ☝️"). The **guess-the-crowd** step is your best engagement idea. There is research behind it: asking people both what they think and what they *expect others* to think gives better answers. In one study this "surprisingly popular" method cut errors by **21.3%** compared with a simple majority vote ([ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2017/01/170125145906.htm)). Nintendo's Everybody Votes Channel used the same "guess the majority" idea. Its first worldwide poll got **500,000 votes in 2 days** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Everybody_Votes_Channel)). Rename the step "Guess the crowd" on everyday polls. Keep the words "exit poll" out of political polls completely. The EVM beep, VVPAT slip, voter ID number, counting rounds and "Result declared" are heavier and more political. Move them into an optional **Election mode**. It stays on for your Modi vs Rahul flagship and for any creator who picks it. Keep your ban on points, levels and streaks. Every app in this space that grew fast and then died (tbh, Gas, BeReal) ran on novelty and had no habit to hold people ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/07/02/facebook-is-shutting-down-hello-moves-and-the-anonymous-teen-app-tbh-due-to-low-usage), [Wikipedia: Gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas_(app))). The answer to that is fresh daily content, not game scores.

## One-tap questions win; show "you vs the crowd" after the vote

**Question formats.** The more effort a question takes, the fewer people answer it. In Pew Research data, people skip closed questions (pick an answer) only **1–2%** of the time. They skip open "write your answer" questions about **18%** of the time, and the gap is bigger on phones ([Pew via Medium](https://medium.com/pew-research-center-decoded/why-do-some-open-ended-survey-questions-result-in-higher-item-nonresponse-rates-than-others-7fabc4a1c328)). On phones, grid questions ("rate each of these in a table") get **25–30%** skipped answers and feel harder ([Mavletova et al. 2018](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0894439317735307)). So add formats in this order, from easiest to answer to hardest:

| Format | What the voter does | Result shown | Build size |
|---|---|---|---|
| Yes / No | One tap | "68% said yes" | Small (works today as 2 choices) |
| Pick one (today's duel) | One tap, 2–10 choices | Bars | Already built |
| Rating / emoji slider | One drag, 5 points (7 at most) | Average plus a small bar chart | Medium |
| Pick several | Tick up to N | % of voters per choice (can add up to more than 100%) | Medium–large |
| Rank | Drag items into order | Points-based ranking (each place gives points, totals decide) | Large |
| Write your answer | Type a short reply | Moderated list | Large, highest legal and moderation risk |

Instagram's emoji slider shows "the current average of how others have responded" as soon as you let go ([Instagram](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/introducing-the-emoji-slider-sticker)). That is the right model for ratings. Keep scales at **5 points**. Research found 5 columns worked best on phones; 7 slowed people down ([Grady et al. 2019](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0894439318773733)). Do not make "write your answer" a format of its own at first. Offer it as an optional "Why? (optional)" box after the vote, so most people can skip it and moderation stays small.

**Results.** The proven order is: vote first, then reveal. X (Twitter) hides poll results until you vote ([X Help](https://help.x.com/en/using-x/x-polls)). This matches your existing "results open after you vote" rule. After the vote, show these from top to bottom. (1) **Your answer highlighted in lime, with "X% chose the same as you"**. Lime already means "you" in your design. (2) Bars for all answers. (3) "Your guess vs the real result". (4) Optional breakdowns (city, age band) behind a tap. Breakdowns need care because nobody logs in. Ask for city, age band and gender only *after* a vote, only once, and always with a "prefer not to say" option. Hide any group with fewer than about **10 votes**. Health statistics bodies hide groups under 10 or 11 for the same privacy reason ([Vanderbilt Data Science](https://www.vanderbilt.edu/data/2026/04/03/small-sample-size-suppression-statistical-disclosure-control/), [ResDAC](https://www.resdac.org/articles/cms-cell-size-suppression-policy)). Never let people combine filters such as city × age × gender. With small groups, someone could subtract numbers and work out how one person voted.

**Avoid these tricks.** India's dark-pattern guidelines (issued 30 Nov 2023) name **13 banned tricks**, including fake urgency and "confirm-shaming", which means guilt-tripping people into saying yes ([PIB](https://pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1983994)). On your site that means: no forced questions before voting, no fake "2 hours left!", no "No, I don't care about my city" button, and no share pop-up after every single vote.

## A daily question plus a WhatsApp-ready result card is the growth engine

**The core loop.** Someone votes. They see a result card made for sharing. They forward it to a WhatsApp group or Status. A friend taps the link, and has to vote to see the results. Then the loop repeats. WhatsApp is the one place that reaches almost every Indian phone. Its Updates tab (Status + Channels) has **1.5 billion daily users** worldwide ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/technology/news/story/whatsapp-rolls-out-ads-and-paid-subscriptions-in-status-and-channels-to-boost-discovery-and-monetisation-480627-2025-06-16)). Wordle shows how far one daily ritual plus a share that does not spoil the answer can go. It grew from **90 players to over 2 million in about three months**, with no app to install ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/05/04/wordle-new-york-times-user-growth)). Your version of Wordle's coloured grid is a card like "My group said Chai, India said Coffee". It says something about the sharer without giving away the result. Build two images. One is a tall 9:16 card for WhatsApp Status. The other is the link preview (the picture that appears when a link is pasted into chat).

**Launch one group at a time.** tbh launched in one US school. Within days about **40% of Georgia high schoolers** had downloaded it, with $0 spent on marketing ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/nikita-bier-got-5m-users-in-2-months-with-a-0-marketing-budget-and-then-sold-his-business-to-facebook-for-30m-he-did-it-twice-b3e0cc1c68)). For you, that means one college, office or WhatsApp group at a time. Start with topics Indians love to argue about. Cricket has a **52%** fan base in India ([YouGov](https://commercial.yougov.com/rs/464-VHH-988/images/YouGov-India-Cricket-Fandom-Report-Feb-2023.pdf)). **81%** of urban Indians name Indian food as their favourite ([YouGov](https://yougov.com/articles/47610-urban-indians-name-indian-as-their-favourite-cuisine-delhi-the-best-place-for-food)). Do not copy the dark side of these apps. Gas earned money by selling hints about *who voted for you* ([Wikipedia: Gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas_(app))). That would break your secret-ballot promise.

**The feed.** Today, Home shows one featured poll plus the newest 12. There is no trending list, no search and no topics you follow ([src/lib/polls.ts](../src/lib/polls.ts)). The new Home should have four parts. (1) **"Today's question"** on top, picked by you. This is the screen's one main thing (its P1). (2) After each vote, the **next poll** appears, so a visit continues without needing points. (3) **Topic shelves**: cricket, food, films, your city. (4) **New** polls. "Trending" can use the open, simple formula Hacker News uses. Recent votes count for more, and every item slowly sinks as it ages ([righto.com](https://www.righto.com/2013/11/how-hacker-news-ranking-really-works.html)). Hidden penalties push down polls that get reported. For "most agreed", use a fair-ranking method called the Wilson lower bound, so that "2 out of 2 agree" does not beat "100 out of 101" ([Evan Miller](https://www.evanmiller.org/how-not-to-sort-by-average-rating.html)). Personal touches can work without login. The phone itself can remember which topics you vote on and hide polls you have already answered.

**Coming back tomorrow.** Web notifications on iPhone only work after the user adds the site to their home screen ([Brainhub](https://brainhub.eu/library/pwa-on-ios)). That makes them weak. A **WhatsApp Channel** that posts the daily question link is a better way to bring people back. So are partnerships with big Indian news or creator channels (Aaj Tak has about 19M followers, per an aggregator: [WhatsScale](https://whatsscale.com/blog/most-followed-whatsapp-channels)). To judge success, use the share of monthly users who also visit on a given day (called "DAU/MAU"). Aim for **20–25% or more**. Benchmarks call 25% "OK" and 40% "good" for social apps ([a16z](https://a16z.com/do-you-have-lightning-in-a-bottle-how-to-benchmark-your-social-app/)).

## Trust and Indian law: a few must-dos apply from day one

*Not legal advice. Get a one-time review from an Indian tech lawyer.*

**IT Rules (platform duties).** Users create the polls, choices and photos on your site. That makes the site an "intermediary" in law, and it keeps its legal shield only if it meets certain duties. The **Feb 2026 amendment** (in force 20 Feb 2026) made the deadlines much shorter. Court or government takedown orders: **3 hours**. Intimate-image or sexual-deepfake complaints: **2 hours**. Ordinary complaints: resolved within **7 days**. Users must also be reminded of the rules **every 3 months** ([Argus Partners](https://www.argus-p.com/updates/updates/update-overview-of-the-it-intermediary-amendment-rules-2026/), [AZB & Partners](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/88502/)). One source gives 36 hours instead of 3 ([IFF](https://internetfreedom.in/it-intermediary-amendment-rules-2026-contradict-their-purpose/)). That figure is probably from the older 2025 rule, so plan for 3. The extra-heavy duties start at **50 lakh registered users** ([AZB](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/88502/)). A no-login site is very unlikely to reach that soon. In practice you need five things: a Terms page listing banned content (in English, Hindi and Hinglish), a privacy policy, a named grievance officer with an email address, an instant alert to your phone for each report, and a one-click "hide" button. You already have the report button and the admin hide switch. One honest risk: a solo owner will find a 2-hour response at night hard. Keeping new photos on hold until you review them (already built) reduces this risk.

**DPDP (data protection).** The DPDP Rules were notified in Nov 2025. Most duties start around **13 May 2027**. The government has consulted on moving this earlier, to Nov 2026 ([DPDP Rules text](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf), [Chambers](https://chambers.com/articles/meity-plans-to-cut-short-dpdp-compliance-timeline-and-notify-cross-border-restrictions-for-sdfs)). A device cookie, an IP address and optional age or city are very likely "personal data". You need a separate, plain-language notice that lists what you collect and why, with a "delete my votes" option that is as easy as voting. Under-18s need **verified parent consent**, and breaking the rules for children can cost up to **₹200 crore** ([PIB](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf)). A no-login site cannot check parents. The safe design is an "I am 18+" tick before anyone gives age, city or gender, or uploads photos, plus no profiling and no targeted ads. Building this now costs little.

**Election law.** Keep the "results sealed" feature and make it stronger. In Bihar 2025, exit polls were banned from **7 AM on 6 Nov to 6:30 PM on 11 Nov**. During the 48-hour silence before voting ends, no opinion poll may be displayed ([PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2182632&reg=48&lang=2)). The site needs four things. A calendar of these windows. Political results sealed everywhere during them: the page, share images, link previews and the API. No "Who did you vote for today?" polls while voting is on. And a "fan poll, not a survey" label on every political poll. Nobody has tested whether a "fun" online poll counts as an exit poll. The research found no ECI action against such a site, so stay well on the safe side.

**Online Gaming Act 2025.** "Guess the crowd" is fine as long as it stays **free**. The Act bans games where people pay or stake money, coins or tokens bought with money in the hope of winning something ([Act text](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/10/8a7f103cefc68ed8aaa2ebc9a2ed7c13.pdf)). Probo, a site where people traded money on yes/no opinions, closed after the ban. Later about ₹117 crore of its assets were attached ([Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/gaming-news/breaking-opinion-trading-platform-probo-shuts-operations-in-india-79357.htm), [Indian Startup News](https://indianstartupnews.com/news/ed-attaches-opinion-trading-platform-probos-rs-117-crore-assets-for-allowing-online-gambling-10907316)). So: never add prizes, paid boosts or coins to guessing, and never offer "predict the election winner" with prizes.

**Defamation and photos.** Polls about *named people* carry criminal defamation risk under BNS s.356 ([LawRato](https://lawrato.com/bharatiya-nyaya-sanhita/bns-section-356)). Allow opinions about public figures ("Who was the better captain?"). Ban accusations ("Did X take bribes?"). Ban polls about private people's looks. Allow photos only of the uploader, or with consent, and add a "this is me, remove it" button.

**One vote per person.** No anonymous web poll can guarantee one vote per person. StrawPoll itself calls its checks "best effort" and blocks VPNs by default ([StrawPoll help](https://support.strawpoll.me/hc/en-us/articles/219430887-Duplication-Checking)). You already have a signed cookie, a database rule allowing one vote per voter, and rate limits. Add three things. Turn on the bot check (Cloudflare Turnstile) for political and brand polls. Use soft limits per network, not hard blocks, because Indian mobile companies put many users behind one shared IP address. And add an "unusual activity" freeze when a poll gets a sudden flood of votes. Show "unverified, for fun, not a survey" on every result and share image.

## Money: sponsored polls first, ads only pay the server bill

Indian display ads pay very little, roughly **$0.3–1.5 per thousand views** (aggregator figures: [Remoby](https://remoby.com/traffic-chart/), [Awisee](https://awisee.com/blog/cpm-rates-by-country/)). As a rough estimate, 1 million monthly voters would bring only about $1,500 a month. Even ShareChat, with hundreds of millions of users, made only **₹723 crore in FY25** ([Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/news/sharechat-posts-rs-723-cr-revenue-in-fy25-cuts-adjusted-ebitda-losses-by-72-10571641)). A better order for a small team is this. (1) Light ads on result pages, plus a "remove ads" tip. (2) **Sponsored polls** sold directly to brands, film studios, OTT and cricket sponsors, for a flat fee per campaign, with a share card and a summary report. Global research panels charge about **$1–2+ per answer** ([SurveyMars on Pollfish](https://surveymars.com/knowledge/pollfish-pricing-analysis-is-it-cost-effective-for-market-research/)), so you can be much cheaper. But never sell your results as a "representative sample". They are self-selected fans, not a scientific survey. (3) A **creator Pro tier** at Indian prices (₹99–299 a month via UPI) with no ads, custom branding and result export. StrawPoll charges $8–52 a month for similar features ([StrawPoll pricing](https://strawpoll.com/pricing/)). (4) Much later, **trend reports** built only from group totals, the way YouGov's data products grew to about 40% of its business ([Research Live](https://www.research-live.com/article/news/sales-of-data-products-deliver-results-for-yougov/id/4011472)). Never sell individual votes. DPDP makes that risky, and it breaks your promise to voters.

## What changes in the code, file by file

The pivot keeps the two most important engineering rules: **one ballot per voter** (enforced by a database rule called a "unique index") and **hidden results never leak**. Everything below comes from a read-only check of the repository on branch `claude/stoic-volta-r2mir5`. Sizes: **S** = under half a day, **M** = 1–3 days, **L** = a week or more, including the three-language text and tests.

**1. Rebrand and words (M in total).** About **75 of the 265** English text keys (about 28%) use election or "duel" wording. Across three languages that is about 215 strings. About 45 are ritual words (VVPAT, ink, exit poll, voter ID, counting, sealed). About 30 just say "duel/मुकाबला/muqabla" ([src/lib/i18n.ts](../src/lib/i18n.ts)). The name "Election" is typed directly into about 8 files: [layout.tsx](../src/app/layout.tsx), [manifest.ts](../src/app/manifest.ts), [TopBar.tsx](../src/components/TopBar.tsx), the two share-image routes ([og](../src/app/api/og/[id]/route.tsx), [card](../src/app/api/card/[id]/route.tsx)), and the calendar file. Put it in one shared setting. Rename `/duels` to `/explore` with a permanent redirect, so old links still work. Keep `/p/[id]`, because every link already shared depends on it. Point the new domain at the site and keep the old `election-three-ruby.vercel.app` redirecting. WhatsApp may show old previews for a while. Internal names like `DuelGame` and the 117 `.duel-*` CSS classes are invisible to users and can wait.

**2. Election mode switch (M).** Add a `theme` field to each poll ("classic" by default, or "election"). The flagship and political polls get "election" automatically. These parts then only appear in election mode: [CastVote.tsx](../src/components/CastVote.tsx) (EVM → VVPAT → ink scene), [InkFinger.tsx](../src/components/InkFinger.tsx), the EVM beep and VVPAT sounds in [sound.ts](../src/lib/sound.ts), counting rounds, swing, "declared", and the "I VOTED" share cards. Classic mode needs its own simple "answer saved" moment so the screen is not empty after voting. The silence window in [silence.ts](../src/lib/silence.ts) and the politics hold in [moderation.ts](../src/lib/moderation.ts) **stay on for all political polls**, whatever the theme.

**3. New question types (L, the biggest job).** Today every count assumes "one voter picks exactly one option" ([src/db/schema.ts](../src/db/schema.ts), [src/lib/polls.ts](../src/lib/polls.ts)). The safe design keeps one `votes` row per voter, so the one-vote rule is unchanged. It adds a small child table, `vote_items`, to hold several picks, ranks or ratings. Each poll gets a `kind` field and a `config` field. Every new column must be added in two places: `schema.ts` *and* the hand-written start-up SQL in [src/db/index.ts](../src/db/index.ts). The 865-line voting screen [DuelGame.tsx](../src/components/DuelGame.tsx) has to be split into a shared shell plus one input part and one results part per question type. This is the riskiest piece, because it holds many edge cases you have already fixed. [CreateForm.tsx](../src/components/CreateForm.tsx) needs a "question type" step. Each new type needs tests for one-ballot-per-voter and no-leak, run on real Postgres as well, as CLAUDE.md asks.

**4. Discovery (M–L).** Today only one poll can be "featured" at a time. Replace that with daily and per-topic editor slots. Add a trending score, page-by-page loading (today there are hard limits of 8, 12 and 60 polls), search, and topics stored in a table instead of the fixed list of 9 ([src/lib/categories.ts](../src/lib/categories.ts)). Show the question type on tiles ("Rate 1–5", "Yes/No") instead of "A vs B". The review page at `/admin` relies on one person. A general platform will need a faster queue.

**5. Rules and docs (M).** The CLAUDE.md "Election rule" ("only things a real election has") and the DESIGN.md opening rule are the product's current core. Rewriting them is **your decision**, not just a text edit. A suggested new rule: "every element must help someone give or read an opinion; no quiz scores". Keep the ban on points, levels and streaks. Keep patricka, the P1/P2/P3 priorities, lime = you, and the three-language rule. Move the old election chapters in [docs/DESIGN.md](../docs/DESIGN.md) and [docs/ABOUT.md](../docs/ABOUT.md) into an "Election mode" chapter instead of deleting them.

## Decisions only you can make

| Decision | Options | Research leans toward |
|---|---|---|
| New name | Hinglish verb or feeling word (*raay*, *bolo*, *chuno*…) vs keep "Election" | A new short name. Check .com/.in/.app domains, Play Store and trademarks first |
| Signature ritual | Keep the inked finger for every poll, or only in Election mode | Keep the ink as the share-card signature for all polls. It belongs to no party and fits any topic |
| Election mode | Flagship only / politics polls only / anyone can choose it | Default for politics and the flagship. Creators can choose it for other polls |
| Modi vs Rahul flagship | Stay as hero / become one featured poll among many | One featured election-mode poll. Home leads with "Today's question" |
| Replace the "Election rule" | New platform rule vs keep it | Replace it, and keep the ban on points, levels and streaks |
| Open "write your answer" polls | Now / later / never | Later. Start with an optional "Why?" box |
| Who runs the 2-hour takedown duty at night | You alone / a helper / auto-hide plus review | Auto-hide on reports, hold new photos for review, and alerts to your phone |
| Hindi and Hinglish everywhere | Keep all 3 languages | Keep them. They are part of the India-first edge |
| Money order | Ads, sponsored polls, Pro, data | Sponsored polls first. Ads only cover the server bill |

## Conclusion

The pivot is less a rebuild than a reframe. The election ritual stops being the walls of the product and becomes its accent: a switch on each poll. The safety rules you built for elections (results sealed in silence windows, the politics hold, secret votes) turn out to be your best protection for a general opinion site, so they should survive the rebrand untouched. The real competitive question is not "election or general". It is "why use this link instead of WhatsApp's own poll?" Your answer has to be the reveal, the crowd comparison and the share card, which is why Phases 1 and 3 matter as much as the new formats in Phase 2.

Three things are still unknown. Nobody has tested whether a "fun" online poll counts as an exit poll under Indian law. Nobody knows how a no-login site would be counted for the 50-lakh-user threshold. And there is no Indian data yet on which taglines or question formats people share most. The cheapest way to learn the last one is to launch the rebrand in a few WhatsApp groups and watch which cards get forwarded.
